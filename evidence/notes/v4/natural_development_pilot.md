# Natural Development Pilot

These are reused V3 excluded cases, not a fresh sample, final evaluation,
population prevalence estimate, or independent additional scene count.

## Full-Wrench Development Adjudication

All 177 replayed candidates with sufficient common-Q duration failed the current
development no-load operating point. The full-wrench B4 baseline agreed on all
177. Primitive geometry was independently reconstructed from recorded quaternion
and center data and compared against the archived floor-clearance signal. No
historical V3 score or label was overwritten. Final calibrated adjudication
remains pending; the other 333 candidates in the complete exclusion inventory
fail unchanged common-Q requirements before this contract distinction matters.

## Two Timing Pilots On The Same 20 Scenes

Twenty unique scenes were selected before branch outcomes, taking the first job
per scene in sorted job-id order from the above replay set. The first pilot used
t0=8 s, H=3 s: nine dependent and eleven unreachable under the preconditions.
For many long controller schedules, 8 s is still the acquisition phase.

A second development timing rule was then recorded before its branch outcomes:
t0 = planned controller schedule end minus 4 seconds, H=3 s. This depends only
on the frozen schedule, not the observed first PASS or a favorable branch label.
The same twenty scenes were used, not twenty new scenes. Warm continuation from
the original full-state snapshot reproduced every recorded target position,
velocity and actuator-force sample exactly before the new branch point.

Results: 17 dependent, 3 both-retain, zero invalid shams or unreachable states.
All sham target position/velocity trajectories were bit equal to the originals.
The three both-retain cases had mean environmental force magnitudes approximately
0.84--0.90 times object weight in the preceding 0.5 seconds, with contact records
present. Thus they are genuine loaded-but-not-necessary examples for this fixed
short-horizon continuation. This does not establish load-free 15-second holding.

The endpoint measures object pose relative to the palm, not world displacement.
A separate kinematic scratch data object computes the post-step palm/target
frame without modifying the branch's data or calling its solver again. A unit
test verifies that matched hand/object translation leaves relative pose unchanged
and that object slip changes it. Control inputs are replayed, not regenerated by
a recovered closed-loop policy.

There are no both-fail or removal-rescues cells in this reached subset. The source
was selected from old exclusion cases; do not generalize the observed fractions
to all grasp attempts. No prediction method was evaluated on a fresh test set,
and no superiority over a simple load rule has been demonstrated.
