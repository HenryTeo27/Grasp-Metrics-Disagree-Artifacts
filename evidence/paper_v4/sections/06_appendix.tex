\section{Implementation and Numerical Qualification}
The observer reconstructs a target-directed world wrench from
\texttt{mj\_contactForce}, respecting the geom-2 force convention. Contact
geometry and force belong to the pre-integration solve state; post-integration
positions and velocities are stored under separate names. Each record includes
an integer physical-step index and measured timestamp. Telemetry is not
decimated for adjudication. Snapshot restoration includes integration state,
warm starts, controls, applied forces and mocap; the command tape supplies the
same remaining inputs on both branches. The branch gate uses prefix conditions
and local $Q$, not whether the future native episode eventually succeeds.

The original 80 calibration controls contain 25 no-load reference passes and
55 failures; B4 and B5 match all 80. There are 57 both-retain, 10 dependent and
13 unreachable development branch states. Thirty-two executions of the first
eight controls vary nominal/half/double timestep and solver iterations; these
are repeated numerical conditions, not 32 new scenes. A separate natural
20-scene development subset has 17 dependent and three both-retain late states;
the latter carry pre-branch environmental load. All 20 labels remain unchanged
under the registered development numerical settings. These intentionally
selected old exclusions do not estimate a natural population prevalence.

The same natural subset is separated by a simple pre-branch load threshold in
development. We therefore do not introduce a new dependency-prediction model or
claim that the integrated auditor improves that prediction. Four additional
single-burst development cases expose 16.9\,s accumulated versus 9.9\,s
continuous unloaded $Q$. They qualify temporal behavior but are not added to
the 80 calibration cases. The final protocol preserves all of these data-role
distinctions.
